# Scoring rubric

Six factors, integer scores, total 0 to 100. The same factors apply to every category. Anchors differ by category where noted. Score what the evidence supports, not the best case.

Hourly rate for time saved: **150** USD. For `career` and `home` candidates, `dollars.low` and `dollars.high` are `hours_saved_per_week x 150 x 50`, using a low and a high estimate of hours.

## Categories

| Category | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `earn` | Makes money now | Micro-SaaS, consulting, freelance work done with Claude, content with revenue, selling templates or agents |
| `career` | Gets Stephen the next role | Job search pipelines, application tailoring, interview prep, portfolio projects that show PM, data, or AI skill, networking tools, skills worth putting on a resume |
| `home` | Home, family, and planning | Meal planning, kids' schedules and activities, travel planning, household admin, budgeting, health and fitness, learning with kids |

Stephen is between roles, so an idea that only saves hours in someone else's job belongs in `career` if it would make a credible portfolio project or sharpen a skill he'd be hired for, in `earn` if it could be sold as a service, and otherwise scores low on Fit.

When a candidate spans categories, pick the one the evidence emphasizes.

## Profile used for Fit

Professional. Senior technical product manager, nine years at Amazon, between roles since January 2026 and looking for a senior or principal PM role in data, AI, or compliance-heavy products. Strongest at 0-to-1 products in regulated or compliance-heavy settings, LLM product delivery (AWS Bedrock, document processing, anomaly detection), data platforms and BI (SQL, pipelines, A/B testing), and stakeholder alignment up to VP level. Comfortable with Python and SQL. Not a professional software engineer, but Claude Code closes most of that gap. Weak spots are video production, cold sales calls, and visual design.

Personal. Parent in Seattle. Active household with a lot of sports. Values hours back on weekday evenings and weekends.

## Factors

### Fit, 0 to 25

| Score | `earn` | `career` | `home` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Needs skills from the weak spots, or a credential he lacks | Unrelated to PM, data, AI, or compliance roles | Does not match a parent household in Seattle |
| 6 to 12 | Learnable in a month, no current edge | Generic job-search advice, or a skill any candidate could claim | Generic, works for anyone, no particular edge |
| 13 to 19 | Uses one strength directly | Shortens his search or demonstrates one strength to a hiring manager | Matches one feature of his situation (kids, sports, planning load) |
| 20 to 25 | Uses two or more strengths, or a compliance or data edge few others have | A portfolio piece or search tool that shows PM plus data or AI skill, or targets the roles he wants | Fits the household closely and uses his data or planning skills |

### Upside, 0 to 20

| Score | `earn` | `career` | `home` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4 | Under $1k a year | Under 1 hour a week saved in the search, no effect on odds | Under 1 hour a week saved |
| 5 to 9 | $1k to $10k | 1 to 3 hours a week, or a small edge in one step of the process | 1 to 3 hours a week |
| 10 to 14 | $10k to $100k | 3 to 10 hours a week, or a clear edge such as a portfolio piece reviewers would open | 3 to 10 hours a week |
| 15 to 20 | $100k or more | Plausibly changes the outcome, such as a stronger offer or a shorter search by weeks | 10 or more hours a week, or removes a recurring family pain point |

### Time to benefit, 0 to 15 (key `time_to_dollar`)

| Score | First dollar or first hour saved |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Six months or longer |
| 4 to 7 | Two to six months |
| 8 to 11 | Two weeks to two months |
| 12 to 15 | Under two weeks. For `home`, the first use already pays off |

### Effort, 0 to 15

| Score | Hours a week to run once set up |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | 30 or more, a full-time job |
| 4 to 7 | 10 to 30 |
| 8 to 11 | 3 to 10 |
| 12 to 15 | Under 3 |

### Evidence, 0 to 15

| Score | What the sources show |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | One anonymous claim with no numbers |
| 4 to 7 | One named person with numbers, or several vague reports |
| 8 to 11 | Two independent reports with specifics |
| 12 to 15 | Three or more independent reports with specifics |

Independent means different people, not one story reposted.

### Durability, 0 to 10

| Score | Will it still work |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Arbitrage or loophole likely closed within months |
| 3 to 5 | Works for a year, then crowded or automated away |
| 6 to 8 | Works for one to two years with ordinary maintenance |
| 9 to 10 | Still works in two years or longer, a durable skill or asset |

## One idea per candidate

A candidate is one specific thing a specific person did with Claude and the result they got. It is not a theme, a category, or a cluster of related reports. Test: could Stephen start doing exactly this on Saturday morning? If the answer needs "well, it depends which one", split it.

| Too vague | Right grain |
|---|---|
| Family logistics and household admin run through Claude | Weekly meal plan and grocery list from the family calendar, generated every Sunday |
| Solo apps shipped fast with Claude Code | Chrome tab manager built in 19 days, 141 users, by talking to Claude Code |
| Small-org back-office automation with Claude | Food bank grant reports drafted from spreadsheet exports with Claude |
| Freelance clients with Claude | Fixed-fee Upwork gigs cleaning client CSV data with Claude Code, about $4k a month |

Titles name the specific idea in twelve words or fewer, concrete noun first.

## Writing the pitch and first step

The pitch is two sentences. The first names what the specific person did and the specific result, with the number when there is one. The second says why it fits this profile.

The first step is one concrete action that takes under three hours, such as "Build a throwaway version for your own household this weekend" or "Reply to the thread's author and ask for their pricing page."
